#24 [20:07–20:22] Factual Error
Claim

Defense argued about a headphone being plugged into Libby's phone while it was underneath Abby. Juror dismissed this, saying liquid/dirt in a charging port causes "ghost touching" (phone screen acting on its own).

The Defense did not "argue" this. Defense expert Stacy Eldridge testified to the phone data and state examiner's report showing that Libby's knowledgeC database recorded wired headphones being plugged in at 5:45 and unplugged at 10:32. Libby's phone screen showed zero signs of damage or ghost-touching when recovered; the examiner took pictures of it working correctly.

Ghost touch is typically a hardware issue (damaged screen, malfunctioning digitizer, poor-quality charger, or moisture). Common fixes include cleaning the screen, removing screen protectors, restarting, or replacing the screen. Libby's phone exhibited none of these conditions.
